What that noise from your water heater means

Water heaters are not silent, but most sounds have a simple cause. Here is what our plumbers look for when a customer tells us their tank has started making a new noise.

Rumbling from the bottom of the tank

A low rumble, especially while the burner or element is heating, is almost always sediment. Minerals in the water settle at the bottom of the tank and trap small pockets of water that boil and burst as the tank heats. Flushing the tank usually stops the noise and restores lost capacity.

Knocking in the pipes

A single loud knock when a tap closes is called water hammer. It happens when fast-moving water stops suddenly. A water hammer arrestor fitted near the valve fixes it.

Hissing or sizzling

On a gas water heater, hissing often means water is dripping onto the burner, either from condensation or from a leak. On an electric model it can mean sediment is covering the lower element. Either way, it is worth having it checked.

Screeching or whistling

A high-pitched sound usually comes from a partly closed valve, such as the inlet valve at the top of the tank. Opening the valve fully often stops it.

When to call a plumber

Call us if the noise comes with a leak, a smell of gas, water that is much hotter than usual, or a relief valve that is discharging. Those signs can indicate a safety problem rather than a nuisance.
