How to protect your pipes before the first freeze

Tacoma rarely gets a long cold spell, which is exactly why frozen pipes catch people out. Homes here are not always built with freezing in mind, and a single night below 25°F can split a pipe in a crawl space or an outside wall.

Disconnect and cover outdoor taps

Remove garden hoses before the first frost. Water left in a hose can freeze back into the tap and the pipe behind it. Fit an insulated cover over each outside tap; they cost a few dollars at any hardware store.

Insulate exposed pipes

Foam pipe sleeves are cheap and easy to fit. Focus on pipes in the crawl space, garage, attic and along outside walls. Close crawl space vents during a cold snap and reopen them afterwards to prevent damp.

Keep the heat on

If you are travelling, keep your thermostat at 55°F or higher. On the coldest nights, open the cabinet doors under sinks on outside walls so warm air can reach the pipes, and let a tap drip slightly.

Know where your shut-off valve is

If a pipe does burst, turning off the main water supply quickly is the difference between a small repair and a flooded home. Find your main shut-off valve now, make sure it turns, and show everyone in the household where it is.

If a pipe freezes

If a tap produces only a trickle in freezing weather, the pipe may be frozen. Keep the tap open and warm the pipe gently with a hair dryer, working from the tap back towards the frozen section. Never use an open flame. If you cannot find the frozen section, or the pipe has split, turn off the water and call us.
